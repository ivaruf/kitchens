# Lemon potatoes — Πατάτες λεμονάτες στο φούρνο

Researched 2026-10-04.

The current recipe is already the dish most Greek cooks would recognise: wedges,
lemon, olive oil, garlic, oregano, a little water, a hot oven, no lid. What the
sources move is the *balance*. Across 19 sources (18 with amounts), normalised to
1 kg of potatoes, the weighted median is about **60 ml lemon juice** (the current
two lemons, ~90 ml, sits at the sharp end), **70 ml olive oil** (current 100 ml),
**~150 ml water** (current 250 ml — the water users' median is 133 ml, everyone
including the no-water cooks 88 ml), **2 cloves garlic** (current 3) and
**2 tsp oregano** (unchanged). Oven 200 °C uncovered, turned once, 1–1¼ hours is
confirmed. Water beats stock clearly, so stock stays a variant; mustard is the
one modern addition used by a large minority (~45 %), and is noted rather than
added because it is not the majority and not in the pantry. The `table` gains
notes for stock cubes, the semolina "crispy trick" and the butter in Vefa's
older version, where before it was empty.

## Method

All counts below are **weighted** (out of 26.5), with raw counts in brackets.

- **Liquid in the tin:** 21.5 (14) add some; 5 (4) add none (#5, #9, #13, #18 —
  the "crispy" school, #13 by parboiling instead). Water wins over stock:
  water 12 (7), stock 3.5 (4), "stock or water" 3 (2), wine 3 (2). → **water**.
- **How much liquid:** two schools that sources name themselves ("crispy" ½ cup
  vs "creamy/brothy" 2–3 cups, #8 and #12). The weighted median of everyone who
  adds water sits at ~133 ml/kg, which is the crispy-but-juicy middle the
  current step already describes ("a little way up the potatoes"). → **150 ml**.
- **Oven temperature:** ~200 °C (190–205) 11 (8); 180 °C 9 (6, several of them
  fan); 220 °C+ 5 (3). → **200 °C (180 °C fan)**.
- **Covered:** foil for the first part 7 (6); uncovered throughout 19.5 (13). →
  **uncovered**. Foil appears where the liquid is large (stock 480–600 ml).
- **Oven time from raw:** weighted median of the uncovered, from-raw, with-water
  sources is 75 min (45–90); the covered ones run 50–105 min. → **1–1¼ hours**.
- **Turning:** of those who say, all turn or stir once (#1, #4, #9, #15 = 8
  weight); nobody says not to. → **turn once, halfway**.
- **Parboil:** 5 (3: #2, #7, #13), all "crispy" variants. → **no**.
- **Peeled, in wedges (κυδωνάτες):** near-universal; #3 and #4 keep the skin on
  small potatoes.
- **When the lemon goes in:** at the start 24 (16); some held back to the end
  2.5 (2: #15, #16). → **all at the start**.
- **Single layer** is stated by #2, #4 and implied by most (a tin, not a deep dish).

Regional and family variants, noted not followed: orange juice alongside lemon
(#3, AB supermarket version, Mononews); bay leaves (#5); rosemary and white wine
with parboiling (Kochilas' yiayia, Visit Greece); mustard-and-honey dressing (the
modern Athens style — Barbarigou, Cookpad, Katerina); cooking in parchment
(λαδόκολλα, #4); Cypriot small new potatoes whole (#4); semolina for crunch (#1).

## Confidence

Strong agreement on the shape: wedges, lemon + olive oil + oregano in every
source, garlic in most, a hot oven (~200 °C), uncovered, turned once, about an
hour. Amounts scatter widely — lemon 20–120 ml/kg and liquid 0–600 ml/kg — because
the sources themselves split into a "crispy" and a "brothy" school; the medians
land in the middle and match the current recipe's intent, just less sharp and
less wet. Less certain: potato weights were estimated from counts ("7 large") for
about half the sources, which moves every per-kg figure by perhaps ±20 %; #16 was
read from a search excerpt rather than the page; #4's water ("3.4 cups") is read as
¾ cup given its "halfway up" instruction; and Vefa's version is second-hand.
Mustard is the honest open question — a fast-growing modern habit (Barbarigou,
Cookpad, Katerina, Nikolopaa) that a recipe written ten years from now might
include.
