# Stifado (beef) — consensus research

Researched 2026-10-04.

The current recipe in `js/recipes.js` is already close to what most cooks make: about as much small onion as meat, the meat browned, red wine and vinegar to deglaze, a cinnamon stick and bay, tomato, and a long gentle simmer. Weighing 17 sources moved five things. **Less vinegar**: 30 ml rather than 50 ml, because the weighted median is 2 tablespoons per kilo and only two pages at the extremes go higher (the one at 280 ml is a different style, with the meat boiled first). **Less tomato paste**: 1 tbsp, not 2. **Less oil**: about 75 ml, not 100 ml. Most Greek home recipes use between a third and a half cup for 1 to 2 kg of meat. **Fewer whole spices**: about 4 allspice berries, not 6. Cloves appear in under half the sources, so they are now optional. **A teaspoon of sugar**, which just over half the sources add to round off the vinegar. The current recipe only mentions sugar as a fix-up in the last `why`. The cooking is also a little shorter: about 1¼ hours for the meat, then 45 minutes with the onions, where the current recipe has 1½ hours plus 1 hour. Sources put the total braise at around 1¾ to 2 hours.

## Method

All counts are **weighted** (native hands-on = 2, ordinary = 1, aggregator = 0.5, total 24). Raw source counts are in brackets.

- **Brown the meat**: browned first 20 / 24 (15/17). Not browned, everything into a clay pot: 1 (1). Boiled first, then sautéed: 3 (2: Cantina, and Mama Vasso, whose Smyrna "light" version is a real regional style). → **Brown it.**
- **Dust the meat in flour**: 5 / 24 (3/17). → **No.** The table note keeps the warning.
- **Peel by blanching or soaking in hot water**: every source that explains peeling does this (1–2 min blanch, or 1–2 h soak in hot water). → keep the blanch step.
- **Onions whole**: whole small onions or shallots 22 / 24. Quartered big onions: #2 only. → **Whole.**
- **Colour the onions**: browned or golden 17 / 24 (12/17). Softened without colour, deliberately: 5 / 24 (Barbarigou in both versions, and #15 which copies her; she says browning makes the dish heavy). Raw into the pot: 1. → **Brown in patches**, as now. The Barbarigou "no colour" school is a real variant.
- **When the onions go in**: **split.** Late, for the last 15–60 min (median ~40 min): 11 / 24 (#3, 4, 5, 7, 10, 12, 13). From the start, with the meat: 13 / 24 (#1, 2, 6, 9, 11, 14, 15, 16, 17, 18). Among the weight-2 Greek home cooks it goes the other way, 8 to 6 for late. → **Late, for the last 45 min**, as the current recipe has it. Several "early" sources warn against stirring so the onions do not fall apart, and adding them late avoids that.
- **Wine and vinegar**: to deglaze after browning in 13 / 17 sources. A marinade first (wine/vinegar/spices, 30 min to overnight): 7.5 / 24 (#2, 4, 5, 14). → **Deglaze, no marinade**; marinating is optional.
- **Spices and paste fried before the liquid**: yes in nearly every source that uses paste (#4, 6, 7, 8, 12, 15, 16, 18). → keep.
- **Covered, stovetop, gentle**: stovetop 21 / 24. Oven: #8 at 175 °C, covered, and #11 in a clay pot at 150 °C (athinorama's copy says 200 °C). Covered: all of them, with a few (#6, #8) uncovering for the last 10–60 min to thicken. → **Covered, lowest simmer; uncover at the end if the sauce is thin.**
- **Total braise**: 1–2¼ h, weighted median about **1¾–2 h**. The current recipe's 2½ h of simmering is longer than every source. → 1¼ h for the meat, then 45 min with the onions.
- **Sugar**: 13 / 24 (9/17) add about a teaspoon. → add 1 tsp with the tomatoes.
- **Serve with**: fried potatoes (#1, 2, 6, 9, 5), rice (#5, 2, 7), pasta (#9, 15) and bread (#7, 17), with Greek sources leaning to fried potatoes. → serve note updated.

**Regional and family variants, noted not followed**: Barbarigou's cognac and nutmeg, with onions softened but not coloured. A Smyrna (Σμυρναίικο) style with the meat simmered in water first, grated onion and orange zest, made with wine *or* vinegar and sugar. A Cretan-site version with raw onions added after an hour. Clay-pot oven versions (γάστρα) with no browning and no vinegar. Rabbit (κουνέλι) and octopus (χταπόδι) stifado use the same onion-wine-vinegar-spice base. Rabbit is often marinated in wine and vinegar overnight, and octopus braises in its own liquid with no water.

## Needs a new pantry item

- **sugar**: about 1 tsp. Just over half the sources use it, and it rounds off the vinegar. The current recipe already reaches for it in a `why`. If a pantry item is unwanted, leave it out of `ingredients` and keep the line in the last step's `why`. The dish works either way.

## Confidence

**Strong agreement** on the shape of the dish. Nearly every source browns the meat (20/24 weighted), uses about as much whole small onion as meat (weighted median ~900 g per kg, which rounds to 1 kg), and uses around 200 ml red wine, a cinnamon stick, allspice, bay, about 400 g tomato and water to cover. Most use vinegar (13/17) but in small amounts. The median of 30 ml is solid, and only the extremes go to 60 ml or more.

**Weaker** on four points. *When the onions go in* is a genuine split, 11 to 13 weighted, with the native home cooks leaning late. *Oil*: many Greek pages just say "olive oil" without a measure, so the ~70 ml median rests on 14 measured sources. *Sugar* is barely a majority, at 54 % weighted. *Cloves* are a minority, at 42 %.

The sources also split along one real stylistic line. One school (Barbarigou and her copies) softens the onions without colour, adds cognac and nutmeg and puts everything in at once. The other browns the onions and adds them late. The consensus recipe follows the second, which the larger and more native-weighted group uses.
