# Melitzanosalata — consensus research

Researched 2026-10-04.

The current recipe is close in shape and slightly off in balance. The sources
agree on more vinegar than we had: a weighted median of 2 tbsp per 1 kg of
aubergine, against our 1 tbsp. They use less oil, about 60 ml against our 80.
They use a little more garlic, 1–2 cloves rather than 1. Lemon is a minority
second acid, so it drops from "½, juiced" to an optional tablespoon.

Parsley is measured at about 2 tbsp chopped, not "a handful". Onion is used by
under half the sources. Where it is used it is raw red onion very finely
chopped, never grated, so it stays optional and its prep changes. Black pepper
is in 15 of 17 sources and is added.

On method:
- Most sources prick the aubergines before cooking, so the new method says so.
- Draining is near-universal and usually 15–30 minutes in a colander or a
  squeezed cloth. That time is now stated.
- The oil is simply stirred in, not beaten in drop by drop. The slow emulsion
  is one lineage, Vefa's "aloifi", not the norm. The current `why` claimed
  otherwise and is rewritten.
- Knife-chopping stays the consensus.
- The cooking method is split. Greek-language and native cooks mostly char
  over a flame. English-language sources mostly roast in a hot oven and often
  finish under the grill. Both are written in, with flame first.

## Method

Counts are given as raw / weighted. The weighted figure counts a native,
hands-on source as 2, an ordinary source as 1 and an aggregator as 0.5.

**How the aubergine is cooked.**
- Oven roast: 10 / 16. #3 and #4 finish under the grill. Typical is
  180–230 °C for 40–60 minutes.
- Flame, charcoal or grill: 7 / 8.5.
- Five of the eight Greek-language sources use flame, and the English sources
  mostly use the oven. The consensus recipe gives both, with flame first.

**Pricked before cooking.** Yes 11 / 16.5. Not mentioned 6 / 8.

**Drained.**
- Yes 13 / 18.5; no 4 / 6.
- Either in a colander or squeezed in a towel or cloth.
- Typical time 15–30 minutes. Stated times range from a few minutes to an
  hour.

**Against browning.**
- 3 / 4 act: lemon on the flesh (#2, #15), or peeling at once into cold
  water (#6).
- Nobody soaks the flesh.

**Texture.**
- Knife-chopped: 9 / 13.
- Fork-mashed: 3 / 5.
- Machine: 3 / 4.
- Not stated: 2 / 2.5.
- Two sources say outright not to purée (#2, #15).

**Garlic.**
- Crushed or pressed: 7 / 9.
- Minced: 3 / 5.
- Blitzed in: 4 / 5.5.
- Mortar: 1 / 2.
- Grated: 1 / 2.

**Acid.**
- Vinegar only: 9 / 12.5.
- Vinegar and lemon: 6 / 10.
- Lemon only: 2 / 2.

**How the oil goes in.**
- Simply stirred in: 10 / 15.
- Whisked into a dressing first: 4 / 5.5.
- Processed with everything: 2 / 3.
- Beaten in drop by drop as an emulsion: 1 / 1.

**Parsley.** In the mix 13 / 18.5; garnish only 4 / 6.

**Onion.** None 8 / 12; raw red onion finely chopped 4 / 6; spring onion
3 / 5.5. None of the sources grate it.

**Serving temperature.**
- Chilled: 5 / 8. Rests of 30 minutes to 3 hours are common.
- Room temperature: 2 / 2.
- Either: 2 / 3.
- Not stated: 8 / 11.5.

**Regional and style variants**, noted and not followed:
- Northern Greek and Macedonian versions add roasted Florina pepper.
- Agioritiki, from Mount Athos, adds walnuts.
- Vefa's "aloifi" is a smooth emulsified spread.
- The creamy taverna and Athenian style adds yogurt, mayonnaise or feta.
- Kochilas has an onion-and-caper salad version.
- Souvlaki For The Soul thickens with breadcrumbs.
- Tahini leads towards baba ganoush and is not melitzanosalata.

## Confidence

**Strong agreement:**
- Garlic: 1–2 cloves.
- Parsley mixed in.
- Draining, and chopping by knife rather than blending.
- Vinegar as the main acid, at about 2 tbsp per kilo.
- No dairy in the plain style: 13 of 17 sources are free of dairy, egg and
  gluten as written.

**Real spread:**
- Oil runs from 20 to 120 ml per kilo. Some cooks keep it low in the mix and
  pour more on top, and the median of about 60 ml sits in the dense middle.
- Charring versus oven is a genuine split, along language lines. Weighting
  favours the oven, the Greek-language sources favour flame, and the recipe
  offers both.
- Onion and lemon each appear in under half the sources, so they are kept
  optional.
- Akis Petretzikis's figures come from a search snippet, not his page.
