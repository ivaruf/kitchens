# Horiatiki — consensus research

Researched 2026-10-04.

The biggest change is the vinegar. The current recipe says flatly "There is no
vinegar". In fact 13 of 17 sources use red wine vinegar, a weighted 18 of
23.5, with a weighted median of about 1½ tbsp for four people. Three of the
13 mark it as optional, and the four that leave it out lean on the tomato
juice alone. So the consensus has a splash of vinegar, and the `why` now
explains that the tomato juice and oil do most of the work.

Other changes:

- **Tomatoes, onion and olives:**
  - The tomatoes drop slightly, to about 500 g (3–4).
  - The onion halves, to about 80 g, which is half a red onion.
  - The olives get a number: about 12 Kalamata.
- **Feta.** It gets a weight, about 150 g, still as one slab and still on its
  own plate for a dairy-free table.
- **Salt.** Only 3 sources salt the tomatoes ahead of time. Nearly all salt
  everything together at assembly, then dress and toss, with the feta laid on
  top. The "salt the tomatoes first" step becomes "salt, dress and toss".
- **Cucumber.** Peeling, or peeling in stripes, outweighs leaving the skin on.
  The recipe now offers "peeled, or in stripes".
- **Capers.** These stay optional; 7 of 17 use them.

Regional variants are noted but not counted:
- **Diane Kochilas, "World's Best Salad".** Cretan dakos style: on a barley
  rusk soaked in orange juice, with capers, kritamo, scallions and no olives.
- **Ioanna Stamoulou, "Υπερ-χωριάτικη".** Adds purslane, herbs, capers,
  kritamo, gherkins and rusks.
- **Giorgos Tsoulis.** A chef's reinvention.

Argiro's own core recipe carries purslane and caper leaves. Capers are
typical of the Cyclades and parsley of the north.

## Method

The counts below are weighted. A native, hands-on source counts 2, an ordinary
source 1 and a commercial or repackaged page 0.5. Each count is given as raw,
then weighted, out of 17 sources with a total weight of 23.5.

**Vinegar**
- Used: 13 / 18. Three of these mark it optional.
- None: 4 / 5.5.
- Red wine vinegar is named in 10.
- No source uses lemon.

**Salting the tomatoes**
- Salt everything together at assembly: 11 / 16.5.
- Salt the tomatoes separately ahead of time: 3 / 3. E9 drains them in a
  colander and throws the liquid away.
- Not stated: 3 / 4.

**Tossing**
- Dressed and tossed, feta on top: 11 / 16.
- Dressed on top and not tossed: 5 / 6.5.

**Feta**
- Always on top.
- Slab or slices: 13 / 17.5.
- Cubed or crumbled: 3 / 5.

**Oregano**
- Dried: 17 / 17.
- At least some on top at the end: about 11.
- Only G1 says to rub it by hand.

**Cucumber**
- Peeled: 5 / 7.5.
- Striped or partly peeled: 3 / 4.
- Unpeeled: 3 / 5.
- Not stated: 6 / 7.

**Onion**
- Red: 13 of 17.
- Untreated: 12 / 17.5.
- Soaked in ice water or pickled: 5 / 6.

**Pepper**
- Green: 16 / 17.

**Olives**
- Kalamata: 11 / 15.5.
- Other: 5 / 6.
- None: 1 / 2.

**Capers**
- Used: 7 / 7.5.
- No source uses kritamo outside the variants.

**Bread**
- Served alongside: 12 / 17.5.
- Rusks go into the salad only in the variants.

**Resting**
- Most sources serve at once.
- 5 sources mention a rest of 10–30 minutes, mostly optional.

## Confidence

**Strong agreement:**
- No lettuce.
- Dried oregano.
- Feta on top, as a slab.
- A green pepper.
- Red onion.
- About 4 tbsp oil.
- About 300 g cucumber.
- Bread on the side.

**Vinegar:** clearly the majority, both weighted and raw. It is still a real
family split, so the recipe calls it "a splash" and says some cooks leave it
out.

**Real spread:**
- Tomato, 240–900 g for four.
- Olives, 4–30 pieces.
- Onion, 15–150 g.

**Weaker data:**
- The Greek-language side is thinner on hands-on native blogs than the English
  side.
- Akis Petretzikis and Serious Eats come from snippets or listings, not their
  own pages.
