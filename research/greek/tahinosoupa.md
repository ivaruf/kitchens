# Tahinosoupa (Ταχινόσουπα), consensus research

Researched 2026-10-04.

**What changed compared with the current recipe in `js/recipes.js`, and why.**

- **More tahini: about 100 g (6–7 tbsp) for 4, up from 4 tbsp (about 60 g).** Both ways of normalising agree on this:
  - **Per serving:** the weighted median is 100 g per 4.
  - **Per litre of water:** the median is about 80 g/L, which at 1.25 L is 100 g.
  - The current amount sits in the bottom quarter.
- **Less rice: 100 g for 4, down from 150 g.** That is the weighted median per serving, and it is also the median per litre (about 100 g/L).
- **Water: 1.25 L.** The unweighted median is 1 L per 4. The weighted median, pulled up by the Greek home cooks, is about 1.3 L. 1.25 L sits between them, close to the current 1.5 L.
- **No cold water to loosen the tahini.** 9 of 14 sources (weighted 12 vs 7.5) loosen the tahini and lemon only with **hot broth, ladle by ladle**, which is the tempering itself. The current recipe's "add a little cold water" step is the minority habit. It is folded into the `why` rather than dropped silently.
- **A short rest is added.** Several sources say the soup looks thin off the heat and sets to the right body within 5–20 minutes (Diamantis, Akis, Epifanios covering the pot).
- **The rice cooks for 20–25 min "until very soft", not 15–20.** The monastery versions cook it well past al dente.
- **Finishing heat: off the heat, or a minute on low.** The sources split evenly (weighted 8.5 vs 8). Unlike egg, tahini cannot scramble, so the recipe allows either.
- **The gluten note is sharpened.** Orzo, fide or hilopites (wheat) are used by 5 of 14 sources, nearly half by weight. Rice still edges it, 10.5 to 9.

## Method

All counts are **weighted** (native hands-on = 2, ordinary = 1, aggregator = 0.5) out of 19.5. Plain n/N is also given.

- **Rice simmered in salted water, not stock.**
  - Water: 12/14. Stock or a cube: Akis, Doriti.
  - Cooking time: Oliveology 15–20 · Food.com 15–20 · GB 15 · Dogma 30 · Mantzi 30–40 · Epifanios "until well cooked". **Median about 20–25 min, until very soft.**
- **Tahini whisked with the lemon first, before any broth.**
  - Yes: 12/14 (15.5).
  - No: 2. Eufrosaini (lemon at the table) and Athinorama (lemon into the pot).
  - Several say plainly that **it seizes into a thick paste** at this point (Epifanios, Dogma, Diamantis).
- **Cold water to loosen the paste before the broth.**
  - Yes: 5/14 (7.5).
  - Hot broth only: 9/14 (12).
  - **Consensus: hot broth only.**
- **Tempering.** Broth goes into the tahini gradually, never tahini straight into the pot: all 14 sources. The amounts are "1–2 ladles", "1 cup" or "3 ladles" (Akis). The target, in Dogma's words, is "like milk, as with avgolemono".
- **Heat after the tahini goes back in.**
  - Off the heat or no further heat: 5/14 (8.5). Epifanios, Diamantis, Eufrosaini, Dogma, Doriti.
  - Brought back to a simmer or boil for 1–5 min: 7/14 (8). Oliveology, GB, Food.com, Athinorama, Foka, Kiros, Akis.
  - **A tie.** The recipe pours off the heat, then allows a minute on low to thicken. Tahini will not curdle like egg, so either is safe.
- **Rest before serving.** Named by Diamantis ("after 20 minutes it will look the way it should") and Akis ("it thickens as it cools"). Epifanios covers the pot.
- **Extra lemon at the table.** Named by Epifanios, Oliveology, Mantzi and Eufrosaini.

**Variants**, noted and not used:

- **Orzo, fide or hilopites** in place of rice (wheat).
- **Mushroom and leek** (Doriti).
- **Grated carrot and parsley** (Athinorama).
- **Tomato paste and olives** (GreekBoston).
- **Blended vegetable velouté** with tahini (mamatsita, mamapeinao). This is a different dish.

## Confidence

**Strong agreement:**

- **The core recipe.** Rice in salted water, then tahini whisked with lemon. It seizes, and it is tempered with hot broth like avgolemono, never stirred straight into the pot. All 14 sources do this.
- **The two lemons** are a clean median.

**Wide spread:**

- **Tahini.** 30–150 g per 4, but both normalisations land on about 100 g.
- **Rice.** 33–267 g per 4. Several sources give no serving count and a "glassful" of rice.
- **Water.** 0.6–2 L per 4. 1.25 L is a compromise between the unweighted and weighted medians.
- **Finishing heat.** A tie between off the heat and a minute on low.
- **Rice versus pasta.** Close, 10.5 vs 9 weighted.

**Gaps:**

- **Fewer big-name English sources exist** than for the other soups. No Kochilas, Dimitra's or My Greek Dish page was found. The count therefore leans on Greek-language home cooks, a monastery and chefs: 14 sources in total, 12 with usable amounts.
- **Akis Petretzikis counts for method only**, because his page was blocked.
