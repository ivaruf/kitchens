# Fava — consensus research

Researched 2026-10-04.

The current recipe already has the right shape. The onion is softened in oil
in the pot, the peas are blended, and raw onion, capers and oil go on top.
What changes is mostly precision.

**Amounts:**
- **Water.** "Cover by two fingers" becomes about 750 ml, the weighted median
  for 250 g of peas (3:1).
- **Oil.** Stays at about 100 ml in total. The sources put roughly 55 ml into
  the pot and the purée and pour the rest on top.
- **Salt.** About ¾ tsp. It goes in with the water, because more sources salt
  at the start than at the end.
- **Bay.** Used by about a third of the sources, so it becomes optional.

**Lemon.** Weighted, about half the sources stir lemon into the purée. Most of
the Greek-language ones instead squeeze it at the table, so the lemon is now
split between the two.

**Garlic.** It shows up almost only in English-language sources (7/16, and 1
of the 7 Greek ones), so it stays out.

**Simmer time.** The weighted median is 50 minutes, so the step wait becomes
"45–60 min".

## Method

The counts are weighted: a native, hands-on source counts 2, an ordinary
source 1, and a reprint or aggregator 0.5. They are written raw / weighted.

**Peas**
- **Rinsed:** stated by 11. Nobody advises against it.
- **Soaked:** not soaked 12 / 14; soaked 4 / 5.5.

**Onion cooked in the pot**
- Sautéed first: 9 / 12.5.
- Simmered raw: 7 / 7.
- Only one source (E5) lifts it out; everyone else blends it in.
- Raw onion on top as well: 11 / 15. "Cooked in and raw on top" is the most
  common pattern.

**Garlic:** none 9 / 11.5; some 7 / 8.

**Bay:** 5 / 6.5. Carrot: 3 / 3.5.

**Skimming the foam:** yes 7 / 9, including 4 of the 7 Greek-language
sources; not mentioned 9 / 10.5. Argiro adds: leave the pot uncovered or it
foams over.

**Salt:** at the start 10 / 12; at the end 6 / 7.5.

**Stirring:** split. Three say don't, three say now and then, three say
often. The recipe says now and then near the end, when it starts to catch.

**Texture:** blended 12 / 16.5 (two also sieve it); by hand 3 / 2; either 1 / 1.

**Lemon**
- Stirred into the purée: 9 / 10.
- Only at the table: 3 / 5.
- None: 4 / 4.5.
- Of the Greek-language sources, only G7 (weight 0.5) blends it in. The
  others serve it at the table or not at all.

**Oil:** a drizzle on top is effectively unanimous. In the pot it goes in at
the sauté (6), at the end (5), or both (3).

**Toppings:** raw onion 11, capers 11, parsley 5, tomato 5, olives 4,
caramelised onion 3, oregano 3. Nobody uses caper leaves.

**Served:** room temperature or cooled is the most common, warm also
appears. Four sources say it firms up as it cools.

**Simmer time:** weighted median 50 minutes, range 20–120. E1's 90–120 is
the long-cook style. Total time about 55 minutes.

Variants, noted and not followed:
- **Fava Santorinis** is PDO since 2011 and is made from *Lathyrus clymenum*,
  not split peas.
- **"Pantremeni" (married) fava** comes topped with onions caramelised with
  capers and tomato (Argiro, Mamalakis, Lucacos, Akis).
- **Ikaria (E9)** uses barely any oil, beats it by hand and serves it cold.
- **Pressure-cooker versions** are on Cookpad.

## Confidence

**Strong agreement**
- The onion is cooked into the pot, and every source but one blends it in.
- The purée is blended smooth.
- Nobody soaks the peas, and the sources that mention rinsing all rinse them.
- It is finished with an oil drizzle, raw onion and capers.
- Water is about 3:1.
- The simmer is roughly 40–60 minutes.

**Weaker or split**
- **Lemon.** Stirred in versus served at the table splits along language
  lines, so the recipe does both.
- **Oil.** The amount in the pot ranges widely, from 0 to 200 ml.
- **Stirring and the lid.** Sources disagree outright.
- **Akis Petretzikis and Mamalakis.** Both were read only through reprints,
  and Akis's method was incomplete.
- **Vefa Alexiadou.** No fava recipe of hers could be found.
