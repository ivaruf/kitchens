# Soufico (Σουφικό), consensus research

Researched 2026-10-04.

**What changed compared with the current recipe in `js/recipes.js`, and why.**

The biggest change is the framing. The current first step says each vegetable is "traditionally" fried before layering. The sources do not support that word.

- Frying every vegetable separately is a minority: 5 of 17 recipes. It is the cookbook and celebration version, from Kochilas's book, Valle, a TV show and Lykavitos.
- Every Ikarian-local voice that describes the dish says the opposite: nothing fried, little oil, no stirring. Those voices are the ikarianews introduction, the Greek gastronomy guide, the Ikarian cooks Sarah Wilson learned from, and the Ikarian blog Marianna. They give that as what separates soufico from briam.
- Counted with weights, "some frying or sautéing" against "raw" is 15 to 12. Among native sources alone it is a 5–5 tie.

So the consensus now **layers raw by default**, and frying is offered as the richer variant.

The rest of the changes:

- **Onions go at the bottom, not the potatoes.** Several native sources put them there to sweeten and catch slightly. Kochilas puts tomato and onion first.
- **One courgette fewer:** 2 instead of 3. The median is 2.
- **One tomato fewer:** 3 instead of 4. The median is about 2.5, roughly 380 g.
- **Less oil:** 120 ml instead of 150 ml. 120 ml is the median both raw and weighted.
- **Shorter cooking:** 40–50 minutes. The median time on the heat is about 40 minutes.

What stays the same:

- No water. 12 of 17 rely on the vegetables' own juice.
- The hob, not the oven: 21.5 to 5.5 weighted.
- A lid.
- Shaking the pot instead of stirring.

## Method (counts are weighted 2 / 1.5 / 1 / 0.5; raw in brackets)

- **Fry or sauté first, or start raw.** Sautéing or frying of some kind 15 (10) against raw 12 (7). Among native sources alone it is a 5–5 tie. The groups break down as:
  - Fry or roast each vegetable separately, then layer: 5 raw.
  - Sauté the onion first, then stew: 3.
  - Sauté everything together briefly: 2.
  - Raw layers: 3, plus the two method-only Ikarian sources.
  - Raw vegetables sweated in their own juice, with the oil added late: 4.
  - Every local Ikarian description names "nothing fried" as the defining rule. That tips the default to raw.
- **Layered or mixed:** 13 against 14 (8 against 9). A tie. Every layered version puts the tomato on top. The order underneath varies:
  - Onion or tomato at the bottom: #4, the gastronomy guide, #1.
  - Potatoes low.
  - Aubergine, pepper and courgette in the middle.
  - #13 orders the layers from slowest-cooking to fastest.
- **Hob or oven:** hob 21.5 (14) against oven 5.5 (3). #1 gives a hob alternative.
- **Covered:** about 11 covered, 3 mostly uncovered, 3 not stated.
- **Water:** none in 12; a splash in 4; "only if dry" in 1.
- **Stirring:** none, or explicitly forbidden, in about 7 (all the layered recipes). Gentle stirring in 7. Shaking the pot in 1.
- **Time on the heat:** median about 40 minutes. The range runs from 15 minutes to 1.5–2 hours (#9, on the lowest simmer). Typical is 30–60 minutes, and frying first adds 20–30.

**Variants**, noted and not driving the consensus:

- **Pie-style**, pressed down and cut in wedges (Kochilas, Hortomania).
- **With wine.** Red is the more common choice.
- **With pumpkin or green beans.**
- **With chickpeas and pomegranate molasses.** A US adaptation.

## Confidence

The sources agree strongly on the vegetable set: aubergine, courgette, mostly green peppers, potato in 85% by weight, onion, garlic and fresh tomato, with no paste. They also agree on about 120 ml of oil, no water, the hob, and tomato on top.

They split on two method points:

- **Frying first or starting raw** is a genuine split. The raw default rests on what Ikarian locals say defines the dish, not on a clear head-count.
- **Layered or mixed** is a tie.

Some of the data is weaker:

- Soufico is a niche dish online. Five sources had assumed servings.
- One source (#13) was read only from a search excerpt.
- #2's introduction says nothing is fried, but its own recipe sautés.
- Kochilas publishes four different versions; she was counted once, using the baked one.
