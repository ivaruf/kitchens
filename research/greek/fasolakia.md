# Fasolakia ladera (Φασολάκια λαδερά), consensus research

Researched 2026-10-04.

**What changed from the current recipe in `js/recipes.js`, and why.** The method already follows the consensus, so the method changes are small. The quantities move toward what Greek cooks actually use, and a few steps are filled in.

**Ingredients**

- **Beans: 800 g → 1 kg.** 1 kg is the most common amount (7 of 17), and the weighted median is about 950 g.
- **Potatoes: "2" → "2 large (about 500 g)".** The weighted median is about 2.4 medium, or 500 g.
- **Oil: 150 ml → 120 ml.** 120 ml is the median both raw and weighted.
- **Tomato: amount added.** It is now given as about 500 g, the median of all forms.

**Method**

- **Water: now a measured amount.** "A glass" becomes about 250 ml, enough to half-cover. The weighted median is 240 ml. Five sources use none, but they are the oil-heavy chef and regional versions.
- **Potatoes go in with the beans, not after.** Potatoes go in at the start in 15 of 16 sources, often fried before the beans. They are now added in the same step as the beans so they get the same coat of oil.
- **A rest before serving.** 7 sources rest the dish for 10–30 minutes, and many say it is better at room temperature.

**Kept as it was**

- Onion first: weighted 25 of 31.
- Beans coated in oil before the liquid: weighted 25 of 31.
- Covered for about 45 minutes: weighted 26 of 31.
- Parsley at the end.
- Reduce uncovered if it is watery.

## Method (counts are weighted 2 / 1 / 0.5; raw in brackets)

- **Hob or oven:** hob 18/18. Barbarigou also publishes a separate oven version.
- **Onion first:** 25/31 (14/18). Two fry the beans before the onion; one cooks them together.
- **Beans coated or fried in oil before any liquid:** 25/31 (13/18). Five give them a real fry of 5 minutes or more. The other eight give a brief coat.
- **When the potatoes go in:**
  - At the start, with or before the beans: 26/28 (15/16). Six of these fry the potatoes with the onion before the beans go in.
  - After the beans have wilted: only #1.
- **Lid:** covered 26/31 (15/18). #2 cooks uncovered throughout; two do not say.
- **Uncovered finish to reduce:** explicit in 11/31 (5/18). Most Greek-language recipes cook to "only the oil left", usually with the lid on.
- **Simmer:** median 45 minutes, range 25–90. #8's 90 minutes comes from his sweat-then-tomato method.
- **Oil timing:**
  - All at the start: 16/31 (10/18).
  - Part held back for the end: 9/31 (5/18), or 11 counting a raw drizzle at the table.
- **Parsley:** at the end in 20/26 weighted. Mint, where used, also goes in at the end.
- **Rest:** 7 recipes rest the dish for 10–30 minutes. Many say it is better at room temperature, or the next day.

**Variants**, noted and not driving the consensus:

- **Crete ("fasoulopatates"):** courgette, 300 ml oil, no water.
- **Barbarigou:** courgette and mint, no water, half the oil at the end.
- **Natsios:** wine, cinnamon and bay.
- **Papazymouris:** sweats the beans for an hour in their own juice before the tomato goes in.
- **Efthymis:** coriander seed, a Cypriot note.
- **English-language versions:** add dill, lemon and chilli more often, and use far fewer beans per person.

## Confidence

The sources agree strongly on six points:

- Cooked on the hob.
- Onion first.
- Beans coated in oil before the liquid.
- Potatoes in from the start.
- Covered for about 45 minutes.
- Parsley at the end.

Oil (120 ml) and beans (about 1 kg) both have clear medians.

Water is where they genuinely split. Five oil-heavy native recipes add none and cook in the tomato and the beans' own juice. Most others add a glass or two. Holding part of the oil back for the end is a sizeable minority (about a third) and was not adopted.

The sources also disagree on how long the beans should cook. Most Greek sources cook them soft, but #8 sweats them for an hour first.

Four sources gave no serving count and were normalised as 4. Tomato weights converted from counts are estimates.
