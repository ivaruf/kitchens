# Fasolada (Φασολάδα), consensus research

Researched 2026-10-04.

**What changed compared with the current recipe in `js/recipes.js`, and why.**

- **Fewer beans: 300 g dried for 4, down from 500 g.** Greek recipes do use "half a kilo", but they say it feeds 5 or 6. Scaled to 4, the weighted median is about 310 g and the range kept is 150–400 g. 500 g for 4 is above every source.
- **Less oil: 90 ml, down from 120 ml.** The weighted median is about 85 ml for 4. The kept range is 47–120 ml; one 240 ml-for-5 white version was dropped. "Plenty of olive oil" is still true. 120 ml for 4 was simply at the top of the range.
- **Tomato paste fried into the base is the tomato.** The grated fresh tomato is now an optional extra. 10 of 15 sources (weighted 17.5 of 25.5) use paste. Fresh, juiced or passata tomato appears in 7 (11.5 of 25.5), usually alongside paste. White versions with no tomato, finished with lemon, are a real minority.
- **No separate boil-and-throw-away step.** The soaked beans go straight onto the softened vegetables, are brought to the boil, and the foam is skimmed. Throwing away the first water is common (5 of 12 dried-bean sources), but the weighted majority (13 vs 7.5) skim instead. The `why` now says both.
- **Salt goes in near the end, not "along the way".** Weighted 10.5 vs 8.
- **The second half of the oil goes in for the last 10–15 minutes, so it binds the broth.** The weighted split is 9.5 vs 8 against stirring it in raw off the heat. The old `why` said "raw oil at the end is what makes it fasolada", which the sources do not support. The Greek word they keep using is χύλωμα, the broth turning creamy. Raw oil at the table stays as a serving note.
- **Parsley is out.** Only 3 of 15 sources use it. Celery leaves do that job in the Greek versions.
- **Garlic and bay are now marked optional.** Garlic is in 5 of 15 (35 % weighted) and bay in 4 of 15 (24 %).
- **A squeeze of lemon at the table is added as optional.** Acid at the end (lemon or vinegar) is in 7 of 15 sources, 47 % weighted.
- **The softening is shorter: 5 minutes, not 10.** That is the median of the sources that sauté.

## Method

All counts are **weighted** (native hands-on = 2, ordinary = 1, aggregator = 0.5). Plain n/N is also given. Bean-handling decisions count only the 12 dried-bean sources (weight 20.5).

- **Soak overnight.** Yes in 10/12 (16.5). The other 2 (MGD and Argiro, both 2) skip the soak and parboil the beans for 30–40 min instead.
- **Boil first and throw away the water.** Yes in 5/12 (7.5): MGD, Argiro, Cantina, sintayes, Marilena. No in 7/12 (13): Lucacos, MiaK, Foodathlon, Kochilas, GCbK, Papafotiou, KK. Kochilas and GCbK **skim the foam** instead. **Consensus: keep the water and skim.** Throwing it away is named as the common alternative.
- **Soften the vegetables in oil before the beans go in.** Yes in 10/15 (16.5). No, everything goes in together, in 5/15 (9). The median softening time is about 5 min (range 2–12 min).
- **Fry the tomato paste briefly with the vegetables.** This is how every source that sautés and uses paste does it (MGD, Argiro, Lucacos, sintayes, WSS, Rappos).
- **Oil in two parts, with some or all of it going in late.** Yes in 11/15 (17.5). All at the start in 4/15 (8).
  - Of the late oil, 6 sources (9.5) simmer it in for the last 5–15 min so the broth thickens. 5 (8) stir it in off the heat. **Consensus: the last 10–15 min, uncovered.**
- **When the salt goes in.** Late, in the last 10–20 min, in 7 sources (10.5). Early or midway in 4 (8). **Consensus: late.**
- **Lid.** On or ajar in every source that says. Ajar: MiaK, Rappos, Marilena. On: MGD, Argiro, GCbK, Papafotiou, and sintayes, which takes it off at the end. **Consensus: lid ajar, then off for the last stretch.**
- **Simmer time after the soak.** The median is about 80 min in total (range 60–120). Kochilas's 2 hours is the long end.
- **Thickening.** By the oil and the beans' own starch in most sources. Mashing or blending a ladle of beans is optional (Lucacos, plus GypsyPlate seen in search). Flour is in MiaK only, and it is gluten.
- **Better the next day.** Said by MGD, KK and others.

**Regional and family variants**, noted but not used:

- **White (λευκή) fasolada** with leek and lemon and no tomato: MiaK, Papafotiou.
- **Western Crete:** orange zest.
- **Northern Greece:** boukovo.
- **The canned-bean weeknight versions** of the diaspora blogs.

## Confidence

**Strong agreement:**

- The vegetable trio (onion, carrot, celery) is in 14 or 15 of 15 sources.
- Overnight soak: 10 of 12.
- Tomato paste as the tomato: 10 of 15.
- Oil in two parts with a late addition: 11 of 15.
- Gentle simmer with the lid on.
- Bean quantity of roughly 300 g for 4, once servings are taken at face value. Greek pages say "500 g" but mean 5–6 people.

**Genuinely split:**

- **Throwing away the first water.** 5 of 12 do. Native cooks fall on both sides.
- **Whether the last oil is simmered in or stirred in raw.** Weighted 9.5 vs 8. I chose simmered, because that is what the Greek-language sources tie to the creamy broth.

**Assumptions to note:**

- **Servings were taken as 4 for Cantina and 6 for sintayes**, because neither gives a number.
- **Kochilas's classic-page amounts** came from search extracts, because a direct fetch of that URL failed; her template page was read in full.
