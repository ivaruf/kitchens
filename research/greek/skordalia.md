# Skordalia (potato) — consensus research

Researched 2026-10-04.

Compared with the current recipe, the consensus is a little gentler and leaner.

**Amounts**
- **Garlic:** about 3 cloves per 500 g potato (weighted median 2.75), down from
  "4, or more". The range is wide and that is said plainly.
- **Oil:** about 100 ml, down from 120 ml.
- **Vinegar:** about 1½ tbsp of wine vinegar, down from 2 tbsp.
- **Lemon:** a minority second acid. "½, juiced" becomes optional.
- **Cooking water:** half the sources use a little of the potato water to
  loosen the dip, so it is written in.

**Method**
- **Potatoes.** Most sources peel and cube them before boiling, which is faster.
  Only 4 of 18 boil them whole in their skins. The cube method replaces the
  skin method.
- **Garlic.** The machine is the most common way to crush it, but the garlic is
  never put through a machine together with the potato. The recipe gives the
  mortar first, then a blender, with the garlic blitzed alone in a little oil.
- **Oil and vinegar.** "Drop by drop, like mayonnaise" is not the consensus.
  Weighted, most sources add them in a few goes or all at once, so the `why`
  is rewritten.
- **Potato texture.** Hand-mashing the potatoes stays. It is the weighted
  majority, and every source that comments on texture warns against
  over-working.

The bread, walnut and almond versions are reported below as variants.

## Method

The counts are weighted: a native, hands-on source counts 2, an ordinary one 1,
and a repackaged one 0.5. Figures are raw / weighted.

**How the potatoes are boiled**
- Peeled and cubed: 10 / 11.5.
- In their skins: 4 / 4. Two of these are Ionian aliada.
- Not stated: 4 / 7.
- Boiling time for cubes is 15–30 minutes, usually about 20.

**Potato type**
- Floury or russet: 4 / 5.
- Waxy or yellow: 1 / 2.
- Either: 1 / 1.
- Not stated: 12.

**Steam-drying after draining**
- G3 says to let them steam 10 minutes or the dip turns gummy.
- G2 says 5 minutes and no longer.

**Garlic**
- Blended or processed: 12 / 15.
- Mortar with salt: 3 / 4.5.
- Knife-minced: 2 / 2.
- Garlic press: 1 / 1.
- Seven sources offer the mortar at least as an alternative.
- Softening tricks: E8 soaks it in lemon; G5 drops it in the potato water for
  the last minute; E6 offers roasted garlic.

**Mashing the potatoes**
- By hand (masher, ricer, fork or mortar): 9 / 11.5.
- Machine: 7 / 8.
- Either: 2 / 3.
- Warnings about a gummy or gluey texture: 3 explicit (E7: never a blender or
  processor; E5: don't overmix; G3: steam-dry or it goes gummy).

**Oil**
- All at once or premixed: 8 / 12.
- Slowly, in a stream: 9 / 8.5. Three of these alternate it with vinegar,
  lemon or broth.

**Temperature of the potatoes when worked**
- Warm: 12 / 13.5. E1 says this is essential, or the dip goes lumpy.
- Cold: 3 / 4.

**Serving temperature**
- Room temperature or warm: 5 / 7.
- Chilled: 2 / 4.
- Not stated: 11.
- Two sources say the garlic grows stronger and the dip firms up as it rests.

**Total time**
- Median about 35–40 minutes. Stated totals run from 25 to 45.

**Variants**, noted and not followed:
- **Ionian and Kefalonian "aliada":** potatoes boiled in their skins, usually
  with lemon, and loosened with cod broth or potato water (G7, G10, Voutsina
  snippet).
- **Thracian walnut and bread** (Kopiaste).
- **Bread only, and walnut and bread** (Olive Tomato).
- **Kochilas's walnut with pita or potato**, pounded in a mortar.
- **Almond and potato**, the American style (E7, E8).
- **Argiro's optional egg.**

## Confidence

**Strong agreement**
- Potato, garlic and olive oil in every source.
- Wine vinegar as the main acid.
- Potatoes worked warm.
- About 100 ml of oil per 500 g, inside a tight core of 86–133 ml.

**Weaker or split**
- **Garlic.** Very wide, from 1.5 to 8 cloves. The median of 3 is a starting
  point; most cooks add more.
- **Machine or mortar.** The weighted counts lean towards the machine for the
  garlic and towards the hand for the potato. The recipe follows exactly that
  split.
- **Oil all at once or in a stream.** Split.
- **Akis Petretzikis.** His recipe was read via a repost, not his own page.
- **Ionian "aliada".** It rests partly on snippets.
