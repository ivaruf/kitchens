# Revithada (Ρεβιθάδα Σίφνου), consensus research

Researched 2026-10-04.

**What changed compared with the current recipe in `js/recipes.js`, and why.** The current recipe has the right shape, but it bakes too hot and too briefly.

- **Oven temperature and time.** The sources centre on 150 °C for about 6 hours (weighted median), clustered at 140–160 °C. The current recipe says 160 °C for 3–4 hours. Shorter bakes appear only in non-traditional or shortcut versions.
- **Oil drops from 150 ml to 120 ml.** The weighted median is about 120 ml, with a cluster at 100–130. A raw drizzle at the end is added instead, which several sources do.
- **Onions are chopped, not sliced.** The quantity stays the same, but most sources chop them finely so they melt into the sauce.
- **The lid gets a seal.** The traditional pot is sealed with a flour-and-water dough rope. Required in 6 recipes and optional in 3 more, so it appears in about half the sources. That seal is gluten, so the recipe uses foil under the lid instead and says why.
- **Optional bicarbonate of soda in the soak.** Weighted, it is slightly favoured (17 vs 11). It is now mentioned as optional.
- **The gluten note is corrected.** The old note said some recipes thicken it with flour. No oven-baked revithada did; the only flour-as-thickener seen was a hob version. The flour that actually matters is the dough seal.

These stay as they were:

- **No pre-boil.** 15 of 17 recipes go straight into the oven.
- **Bay leaves.** 12 of 17 use them, median 2.
- **Lemon only at the end.** 15 of 17 use lemon, and none add it at the start. One chef says early lemon "tightens" the chickpeas.

## Method (counts are weighted 2 / 1 / 0.5; raw in brackets)

- **Soak overnight:** 28 (17/17). Soft or bottled water is stressed by the island sources. A salted soak appears in 3.
- **Bicarbonate in the soak, then rinse and rub off the skins:** 17 (9) yes vs 11 (8) no.
- **Pre-boil on the hob before the oven:** 2.5 (2) yes vs 25.5 (15) straight into the oven.
- **Salt at the start:** 25.5 (15) vs late 2.5 (2).
- **Vessel.** Clay (skepastaria, tsikali or gastra) is the first choice in 15. A Dutch oven or cast iron is the usual alternative. Several sources say to use a pot the chickpeas nearly fill.
- **Covered:** 17/17. Breakdown:
  - Dough seal required: 12 (6).
  - Dough seal optional: 5 (3).
  - Lid only: 10.5 (7).
  - Parchment and foil: 0.5 (1).
- **Water level.** Two schools:
  - "Tilt to the lip, chickpeas just covered": 6 recipes, mostly island ones.
  - Covered by 2–4 cm: most of the rest.
- **Oven temperature (main phase):** median 150 °C, raw and weighted. Range 90–180 °C, with a cluster at 140–160 °C. Starting variations:
  - Cold start: 3 recipes.
  - Hot start, then lowered: 3 recipes.
- **Bake time:** median 6 h. Range 3–12 h; the overnight options run 80–90 °C for 12 h.
- **Lemon:** at the end or at the table in 14. None add it at the start. #8 warns that early lemon toughens the chickpeas.
- **Finish.** Most target a creamy dish with little liquid, not a soup. Finishing touches:
  - A raw olive oil drizzle at the end: 3 sources.
  - A five-second blend with a stick blender: 1 source.

**Variants**, noted and not driving the consensus:

- **Sifnos purist** (#2, #11): chickpeas, onion, oil, salt, pepper and nothing else. Baked overnight in the bakery's wood oven after Saturday's bread.
- **Paros:** garlic, tomato and rosemary.
- **Kythnos:** celery, carrot, cinnamon and allspice.
- **Kochilas's book version:** orange, honey and tomato.

## Confidence

The sources agree strongly on these points:

- Overnight soak.
- No pre-boil.
- Salt at the start.
- A lidded clay or heavy pot.
- About 2 large onions per 500 g chickpeas.
- Lemon only at the end.
- A low oven around 150 °C for most of a day.

Oil is the main disagreement. The middle cluster is 100–130 ml, but four native sources use 180–270 ml. The other unresolved points are the dough seal, which about half use, and bicarbonate in the soak, which is close to a coin toss.

Coverage has gaps. None of the major English sites has the dish, so the set leans Greek-language, which suits the weighting. Three sources needed approximate conversions: water-glass oil amounts, and onion counts converted to grams.
